**FACTION DESIGN**  
  
**NEW CARTHAGE ENCLAVE**  
  
**Resources:** Logic / Alloy  
  
**Doctrine:** REGULATE  
  
**Central Question:**  
  
**How much freedom can civilization survive?**  
  
New Carthage believes the Sunder did not destroy civilization because humanity lacked intelligence.  
  
It destroyed civilization because humanity failed to control what its intelligence created.  
  
Its cards therefore manipulate:  
  
* legality  
* restrictions  
* permissions  
* authority  
* prediction  
* infrastructure  
* Warden oversight  
* controlled access  
* institutional memory  
  
New Carthage should rarely ask:  
  
“Can I destroy you?”  
  
It should ask:  
  
**“Are you still allowed to do that?”**  
  
⸻  
  
**001 — CIVIC ENFORCER**  
  
**Faction:** New Carthage  
**Type:** Operative  
**Subtype:** Human  
**Value:** Conviction  
**Trait:** Technopathic  
  
**Combat Profile**  
  
A disciplined enforcement Operative designed to make the opponent reconsider a commitment.  
  
**Rules Text**  
  
**Whenever an opposing player activates a targeted effect during your turn, you may pay 1 Logic. If you do, restrict one legal target of that effect until the next State Check.**  
  
**V5.8 Decision**  
  
Spend Logic to restrict the opponent’s choice now, or preserve Logic for a more dangerous response.  
  
**Counterplay**  
  
The opponent can:  
  
* choose another legal target;  
* respond before the restriction resolves;  
* remove Civic Enforcer;  
* wait for a better timing window.  
  
**Story Residue**  
  
The enforcement itself can become the beginning of a remembered conflict between authority and autonomy.

**NEW CARTHAGE — FACTIONAL DESIGN SUMMARY**  
  
The faction’s mechanical language is now:  
  
**RESTRICT → RECORD → PRESERVE → ADMINISTER**  
  
Its deepest strategic tension is:  
  
**ORDER vs AUTONOMY**  
  
Its most important Warden question is:  
  
**When does protection become authority?**  
  
Its most important Character Value is:  
  
**CONVICTION**  
  
because New Carthage is fundamentally concerned with who has the right to decide.  
  
Its secondary Value is:  
  
**COGNITION**  
  
because regulation requires information.  
  
Its characteristic Traits are:  
  
* Technopathic  
* Memetic  
  
Its strongest system relationships are:  
  
**Warden + Conviction + Historical State**  
  
and:  
  
**Sector + Logic + Regulation**  
  
⸻  
  
**NEW CARTHAGE’S V5.8 PLAY EXPERIENCE**  
  
A successful New Carthage player should feel:  
  
“I saw the danger coming.”  
  
Then:  
  
“I restricted the choice.”  
  
Then:  
  
“They found another way.”  
  
Then:  
  
“So I recorded it.”  
  
And several turns later:  
  
“You did this before.”  
  
The faction should therefore not simply feel like a control deck.  
  
It should feel like an **institution that remembers**.  
  
Its greatest strength is not preventing every action.  
  
Its strength is making every decision leave a record.

**FACTION II — OMEGA FOUNDRY SWARM**  
  
**Cards 025–048**  
  
⸻  
  
**FACTION DESIGN**  
  
**OMEGA FOUNDRY SWARM**  
  
**Resources:** Bio-Matter / Alloy  
  
**Doctrine:** EVOLVE  
  
**Central Question:**  
  
**How much can you change before you are no longer yourself?**  
  
Omega Foundry does not regard mutation as failure.  
  
Mutation is information.  
  
Damage is information.  
  
Death is information.  
  
Reconstruction is information.  
  
The Foundry’s philosophy is simple:  
  
**If the organism survives, the organism has learned.**  
  
Its cards therefore interact heavily with:  
  
* Integrity  
* Bio-Matter  
* Alloy  
* Traits  
* Mutation  
* Reconstruction  
* Echoes  
* Warden Hosts  
* Humanity  
* Cognition  
* Body/Memory/Intelligence  
  
Unlike New Carthage, Omega Foundry does not primarily restrict choices.  
  
It makes choices **irreversible enough to matter**.

A successful Omega player should feel:  
  
“I can survive this.”

“I can build another one.”  
  
The critical question should never be merely:

**“How much can I change before the thing I am protecting stops being the thing I saved?”**  
  
That question is what separates Omega Foundry from a conventional mutation deck.  
  
The Foundry does not merely evolve creatures.  
  
It makes **identity itself a strategic resource**.

**PRESERVATION**  
  
**MEMORY**  
  
**IDENTITY**

Every damaged Operative should create another:  
  
**Can this person still survive?**  
  
Every Echo should create the deepest one:  
  
**If I remember them, what exactly have I preserved?**

**SALVAGE IS NEVER PERFECT.**

That is the Waste-Walkers.

**HUMANITY**  
  
because detachment is ultimately about preserving the person rather than the systems surrounding them.

* Neural  
* Technopathic

and:  
  
**Voluntary refusal + Echo**

**THE ASCETIC PARADOX**  
  
The Monks introduce one of the most important philosophical tensions in **Synapse & Steel**:  
  
**Can refusing control become another form of control?**  
  
At first, the answer appears simple.  
  
The Monks refuse:  
  
* the Warden;  
* the Market;  
* Hardware;  
* information;  
* resource accumulation;  
* technological dependence.  
  
But the deeper the player enters the faction, the more complicated it becomes.  
  
The Monks can begin to derive **power from refusing power**.  
  
That is intentional.  
  
⸻  
  
**V5.8 ASCETIC DESIGN AXIOM**  
  
**DETACHMENT MUST ALWAYS COST SOMETHING.**  
  
If detachment were simply upside, the faction would become a generic anti-meta strategy.  
  
Instead:  
  
**REFUSE**  
  
↓  
  
**GAIN INDEPENDENCE**  
  
↓  
  
**LOSE ACCESS**  
  
↓  
  
**BECOME HARDER TO CONTROL**  
  
↓  
  
**BECOME LESS CAPABLE OF USING THE SYSTEM**  
  
The player must continually decide:  
  
**How much of civilization am I willing to abandon in order to remain free from it?**  
  
That is the Ascetic Monks.

**HISTORY DOES NOT CREATE CONSENT.**  
  
A previous agreement never automatically forces a future decision.  
  
That distinction is essential to keeping the faction strategically oppressive without making it mechanically deterministic.

**“That doesn’t mean I have to agree again.”**  
  
That is the Corporate Hegemony’s defining conflict.  
  
It does not merely control information.  
  
It attempts to turn **knowledge of past choices into ownership of future choices**.  
  
The rules deliberately prevent that final step.  
  
That is where the player’s agency lives.

**SCRAP-METAL ZEALOTS — FACTIONAL DESIGN SUMMARY**  
  
The faction’s mechanical language is:  
  
**SACRIFICE → DESTROY → IGNITE → REBUILD**  
  
Its deepest strategic tension is:  
  
**SACRIFICE vs PRESERVATION**  
  
Its primary Character Value is:  
  
**CONVICTION**  
  
because sacrifice only has meaning when it is chosen.  
  
Its secondary Value is:  
  
**HUMANITY**  
  
because the faction repeatedly asks whether a person can legitimately be spent for a greater purpose.  
  
Its supporting Value is:  
  
**COGNITION**  
  
because the player must understand what their sacrifice creates.  
  
Its characteristic Traits are:  
  
* Technopathic  
* Neural  
* Magnetic  
  
Its strongest system relationships are:  
  
**Sacrifice + Alloy + Combat**  
  
and:  
  
**Destruction + Historical State**  
  
and:  
  
**Sacrifice + Echo + Reconstruction**  
  
⸻  
  
**THE ZEALOT PARADOX**  
  
The Zealots claim:  
  
**Nothing has value until you are willing to lose it.**  
  
But the game creates a deeper problem.  
  
If everything becomes fuel, eventually there is nothing left to burn.  
  
This is intentional.  
  
The faction therefore rewards:  
  
**short-term sacrifice**  
  
for:  
  
**long-term transformation.**  
  
But reckless sacrifice can leave the player with:  
  
* no Hardware;  
* no Sectors;  
* no protection;  
* weakened Operatives;  
* reduced future options.  
  
The player must decide whether the thing they are burning is:  
  
**fuel**  
  
or:  
  
**the foundation they actually needed.**  
  
⸻  
  
**V5.8 ZEALOT DESIGN AXIOM**  
  
**SACRIFICE MUST CREATE SOMETHING.**  
  
A sacrifice that simply removes a permanent is not enough.  
  
It should produce at least one of:  
  
* immediate power;  
* information;  
* historical memory;  
* resource acceleration;  
* positional advantage;  
* reconstruction opportunity;  
* future strategic leverage.  
  
But:  
  
**THE THING SACRIFICED MUST MATTER.**  
  
If the player can sacrifice meaningless tokens indefinitely, the faction loses its identity.  
  
The strongest Zealot decisions should therefore involve real objects:  
  
**A Sector for lethal pressure.**  
  
**Hardware for survival.**  
  
**An Operative for acceleration.**  
  
**Memory for future reconstruction.**  
  
**Identity for power.**  
  
⸻  
  
**SCRAP-METAL ZEALOTS PLAY EXPERIENCE**  
  
A successful player should feel:  
  
“I can save this.”  
  
Then:  
  
“But if I sacrifice it, I can do something much bigger.”  
  
Then:  
  
“I’m choosing to lose it.”  
  
And later:  
  
“I remember what I sacrificed to get here.”  
  
That final sentence is the faction’s emotional center.  
  
The Zealots are not simply an explosive faction.  
  
They are the faction that asks:  
  
**What are you willing to burn to become something else?**

The Void-Walkers appear to possess the ultimate freedom:  
  
**They can disappear.**  
  
But disappearance has consequences.  
  
A Phased Operative may avoid:  
  
* damage;  
* targeting;  
* combat;  
* restrictions.  
  
But it may also lose:  
  
* combat participation;  
* opportunities;  
* interactions;  
* access to other effects.  
  
Therefore:  
  
**Phase is not invulnerability.**  
  
It is a decision.  
  
The player must ask:  
  
**Is remaining here more dangerous than becoming temporarily absent?**  
  
⸻  
  
**PHASE-STATE DESIGN LIMITS**  
  
The v5.8 Void-Walker cards intentionally preserve several hard boundaries.  
  
**1. Phase does not equal removal**  
  
A Phased permanent still exists in the game state.  
  
**2. Phase does not erase history**  
  
The game can still preserve its previous state.  
  
**3. Phase does not automatically sever a Warden**  
  
Interaction across Phase-State simply requires an effect that permits it.  
  
**4. Phase does not automatically preserve combat**  
  
An Operative that phases out of combat remains removed from that combat unless another effect says otherwise.  
  
**5. Phase does not create infinite protection**  
  
Most Phase effects operate only until the next State Check or impose a meaningful cost.  
  
**6. Phase does not create identity**  
  
An entity being difficult to locate does not make its identity unknowable unless an effect establishes that uncertainty.  
  
These limits keep the mechanic powerful without allowing it to replace the entire game.  
  
⸻  
  
**V5.8 VOID-WALKER DESIGN AXIOM**  
  
**FREEDOM REQUIRES THE ABILITY TO LEAVE.**  
  
**STABILITY REQUIRES THE ABILITY TO REMAIN.**  
  
The Void-Walker must continually choose between:  
  
**BE HERE**  
  
and:  
  
**BE UNREACHABLE**  
  
The faction becomes strongest when those choices conflict.  
  
⸻  
  
**VOID-WALKER PLAY EXPERIENCE**  
  
A successful player should feel:  
  
“You had me.”  
  
Then:  
  
“No—you had where I was.”  
  
Then:  
  
“I’m somewhere else.”  
  
But eventually:  
  
“If I keep disappearing, I cannot participate either.”  
  
And that produces the faction’s deeper question:  
  
**If you are always free to leave, what does it mean to remain?**  
  
That is the Void-Walkers.

**THE SUNDER ECOSYSTEM DESIGN PRINCIPLE**  
  
The neutral Sunder cards use six recurring concepts:  
  
**CORRUPTION**  
  
The environment becomes unstable.  
  
**ENTROPY**  
  
Something must be spent to keep things functioning.  
  
**ECHO**  
  
Information survives its original context.  
  
**PHASE**  
  
Reality temporarily loses certainty about presence.  
  
**HISTORICAL STATE**  
  
The game remembers.  
  
**TETHER**  
  
Human and machine remain connected without becoming identical.  
  
These are not separate subsystems.  
  
They are expressions of the same underlying idea:  
  
**THE WORLD REMEMBERS WHAT HAPPENED, EVEN WHEN THE PEOPLE WHO CAUSED IT ARE GONE.**  
  
⸻  
  
**THE SUNDER IS NOT A FACTION**  
  
This distinction is important.  
  
The Sunder Ecosystem should never behave like an eighth faction.  
  
It has:  
  
* no Doctrine;  
* no Character Value;  
* no faction resource identity;  
* no Warden philosophy;  
* no victory condition of its own.  
  
Instead, it creates circumstances in which every faction’s philosophy is tested.  
  
New Carthage asks:  
  
Can order contain this?  
  
Omega asks:  
  
Can it evolve?  
  
Waste-Walkers ask:  
  
Can we survive it?  
  
Ascetic Monks ask:  
  
Can we detach from it?  
  
Corporate Hegemony asks:  
  
Can we measure and own it?  
  
Scrap-Metal Zealots ask:  
  
What can we destroy to overcome it?  
  
Void-Walkers ask:  
  
Can we simply leave it?  
  
The Sunder answers none of these questions.  
  
It creates the question.  
  
⸻  
  
**NEUTRAL DESIGN AXIOM**  
  
**THE WORLD SHOULD SOMETIMES BE THE OPPONENT.**  
  
But the world must remain predictable enough to play against.  
  
Therefore, Sunder effects should generally:  
  
* create pressure;  
* create uncertainty;  
* force decisions;  
* preserve consequences;  
* interact with existing systems.  
  
They should not:  
  
* introduce another resource;  
* create another gauge;  
* create another Character Value;  
* create another turn structure;  
* bypass the Stack without justification;  
* invalidate faction identity;  
* make ordinary decisions meaningless.  
  
The Sunder is dangerous because it changes the circumstances of the decision.  
  
Not because it removes the player’s agency.  
  
⸻  
  
**THE STORY RESIDUE OF THE SUNDER**  
  
The most important neutral cards are those that leave something behind.  
  
A destroyed Operative can leave:  
  
**Memory.**  
  
A corrupted Sector can leave:  
  
**Historical State.**  
  
A Phased permanent can leave:  
  
**A record of absence.**  
  
A Warden disagreement can leave:  
  
**Evidence that someone once chose differently.**  
  
The battlefield therefore becomes an accumulating history.  
  
At the beginning of a game, the table represents:  
  
**A civilization.**  
  
By the end, it should represent:  
  
**The evidence of what happened to one.**  
  
That is the Sunder.
